# Mashups: combine by system ownership

Read this only when the idea joins two or more chipsets (the one-shot card's A mashup). A
mashup takes every joined genre's lines, then settles each system on its own: one genre need not
run the whole game. Each sheet's In a mashup paragraph says what that genre leads.

## What each parent brings

The brief's Look and references reads as both parents: what each brings to the look (the
figures, the ground or the palette its players know it by) and to the screen (the part its
players reach for first), with a game of each parent among the References. The system each
leads is on the Leads line.

The seam is where the parents meet: the one moment where one parent's verb changes the other's
system. The brief's First minute ends on it, so the build makes the seam work before either
parent alone. Where the parents meet, for example:

- `rhythm` + `action-roguelite`: an attack that lands on the beat fills the meter that picks the
  run's next song; the beat track and the build's picks share one screen.
- `platformer` + `grid-puzzle`: a jump lands on a tile that moves the puzzle's pieces; the
  level's goal and the moves left sit beside the player.
- `turn-based-strategy` + `management-tycoon`: a worked tile's goods reach a market whose
  prices a law changes; the map's yields and the market panel are one turn apart.

## Who leads which system

The person's words lead first. Without them:

- **Input and camera:** the genre whose verb the player repeats most.
- **Death, checkpoints and saving:** the genre whose stakes the idea wants.
- **Progression, builds and economy:** the genre whose decisions shape the run or the day.
- **Rules:** what a move does, what wins, undo: the genre that owns the puzzle or the duel.
- **Enemies and threat:** the genre whose danger the idea is about.
- **Structure:** levels, runs, days or one open place: the genre that sets the session's shape.
- **Who acts and who talks**, when more than one person plays: the genre with the social format.

A look, a theme or a setting decides none of these.

A system named on the brief's Leads line takes that genre's lines; the other genre's lines about
it are left out. Never drop a genre's lines wholesale.

## Known mashups

- `platformer` + `grid-puzzle` or `logic-puzzle`: the platformer leads movement and camera; the
  puzzle leads rules, level teaching, undo and checking a solution. Watch: forgiving movement
  never lets a jump skip the puzzle. (`puzzle-platformer` is this blend as one sheet.)
- `metroidvania` + `detective`: the metroidvania leads traversal and the joined map; the
  detective leads evidence and what knowing opens. Watch: tell an ability you lack from a rule
  you could already work out.
- `action-rpg` or `soulslike` + `character-action`: action leads timing, targeting and camera;
  the RPG leads builds, levels and gear; the stakes lead checkpoints. Watch: hits and survival
  still matter after upgrades.
- `twin-stick-shooter` + `action-roguelite` or `survivors-like`: shooting and movement lead the
  feel; combinations lead the build; the run leads what is kept and what is lost. Watch: aim and
  threats stay readable as combinations grow.
- `roguelike-deckbuilder` + `tactical-rpg`: tactics leads space and enemy intent; the deck leads
  draw, removal, exhausting and the build. Watch: show exactly what an action does, and tell a
  card spent for the fight from one gone for good.
- `city-builder` + `action-roguelite`: the city leads placement, workers, services and what is
  going wrong; the run leads endings and what carries over. Watch: a clear ending without a
  throwaway city.
- `survival-crafting` + `cozy-cleanup` or `sandbox`: survival leads needs and danger out there;
  cozy building leads placement, decorating and making it yours. Watch: the person chooses how
  much danger reaches home.
- `farming-life-sim` + `management-tycoon`: the life sim leads the day and the people;
  management leads the flow of goods and what is going wrong. Watch: stopping after a day stays
  safe.
- `sandbox` or `stealth` + `action-adventure` (an open-world immersive sim): the simulation leads
  interactions that always work the same and many ways to solve; the open world leads landmarks,
  how full it is, schedules and what stays changed. Watch: a big map is no stand-in for systems
  that interact. A systemic idea: the card's Skeleton line applies.
- `stealth` + `physics-puzzle` (a heist with destruction): stealth leads what is known and being
  seen; the heist leads planning and committing; destruction leads changing the route. Watch:
  make the switch from plan to escape clear, and keep what the player broke.
- `survival-horror` + `first-person-shooter`: horror leads what you can know and the tension;
  the shooter leads aim and gun feedback; survival leads scarcity and the inventory. Watch: never
  stack every mercy rule, or every penalty for being noticed.
- `co-op` + `survival-horror` or `party-game`: horror leads the nearby threat; co-op leads
  talking, rescue, each player's part and getting back up. Watch: comedy needs consequences
  everyone can follow.
- `extraction` + `action-rpg`: extraction leads what is kept or lost; the shooter leads combat;
  the RPG leads gear and its worth; trusted trades wait for online play (needs a server: not built yet). Watch: owning,
  losing and getting back are each explained on their own.
- `racing` + `action-rpg` or `sandbox`: racing leads handling and fair time trials; the RPG
  leads progression; the sandbox leads things to do in the world. Watch: catch-up rules in races
  stay out of time trials.
- `arcade-sports` + `fighting`: sports leads scoring and the flow of play; fighting leads
  command input, recovery, defence and competing. Watch: help and input tolerance are the same
  for both sides, and both can see them.
- `rhythm` + `character-action` or `beat-em-up`: rhythm leads timing judgement; action leads
  quick input and what a hit does; the sound follows the music's clock. Watch: never delay input
  so that visuals land on the beat.
- `logic-puzzle` or `grid-puzzle` + `visual-novel` or `walking-sim`: the puzzle leads rules that
  hold; the story leads what a repeated action means. Watch: teach a control before its meaning
  changes.
- `turn-based-strategy` + `visual-novel` (strategy with character drama): strategy leads economy
  and territory; the characters lead stress, bonds and events. Watch: what the player knows
  stays apart from what a character knows, and each consequence can be traced.
- `level-maker` + `physics-puzzle` or `sandbox`: the editor leads editing, undo, what depends on
  what, and checking a level before it is shared; physics leads the shared rules; who may edit
  waits for online play (needs a server: not built yet). Watch: a maker's own clear proves a route exists, not that it is
  fair.
- `sailing` + `survival-crafting` + `co-op`: sailing leads wind, floating, the boat's motion and
  aiming; survival leads exposure and supplies; co-op leads upkeep and talking. Watch: the water
  the player sees is the water that moves the boat.
- `cozy-cleanup` + `detective` + `visual-novel`: cozy play leads forgiving handling and finishing
  without pressure; deduction leads evidence; the story leads what belongings and places mean.
  Watch: hints keep the reasoning, and a forgiving finish never erases the puzzle.

## Conflicts

When two genres want opposite things, the brief settles it in the system's Leads entry: what
each wanted and what this game does.

- **Heavy commitment against instant cancels:** choose per move, and show when a move can be
  cancelled.
- **Hidden help against fair competition:** help fitted to the device and the mode, said openly
  where players compete.
- **Failure that matters against easy retries:** say what resets, what stays, and how a session
  can safely end.
- **Helpers against a party that plays itself:** keep a hit, a save or a finishing blow for the
  player only where the idea needs it.
- **Endless growth against readable numbers:** cap the arithmetic and the loops that trigger
  each other, and keep the big payoff builds.
- **Not knowing against planning that can be solved:** say what is shown, committed, left to
  chance, or hidden.
- **A camera for atmosphere against player control:** framing the player can take over, with
  every warning still in view.
- **Spectacle in order against quick replays:** keep the order of events, and let them speed up
  or skip.
- **Looking forward to tomorrow against pressure to keep playing:** a clear saved state, and
  reminders that wait.
- **Free building against checked challenges:** check completion where finishing is the point;
  leave free building free.

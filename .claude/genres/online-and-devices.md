# Online play and other devices: what a one-shot builds

Read when the card is made, and only when the person's words ask for online play, VR, a phone or
a console. A one-shot is one browser on one machine: nothing here is played over a network or on
the device itself, and the card says so in plain words before anything is built.

## Online play: name the level

Online play comes in five levels. Name the one their words ask for on the card's Online line, in
its plain words, with what this one-shot builds instead. When the genre's sheet has an Online
line, its stand-in is the one for that genre; this table names the level.

| Level | What they ask for | What the one-shot builds instead |
|---|---|---|
| on one screen | friends playing together at one screen or keyboard | that, as asked: it is not online at all |
| async | leaderboards, ghosts, daily challenges, turns taken over days | a daily seed from the date with a code to share it, a local leaderboard, a ghost of your own best run |
| session | a match or co-op with friends on their own machines | players on one screen, and bots in the empty seats, named as bots |
| dedicated servers | ranked or competitive play against strangers | bots in the seats, named as bots, with lag only as a slider |
| persistent world | a world many players share at once, an MMO | bot players in this browser, named as bots, and the character saved here |

- **The card's line:** `Online: <the level>: needs a server, so not built; built instead: <the
  stand-in>`. On one screen has no Online line: it is built as asked.
- **More than one level** (a co-op game with a leaderboard): one Online line, the highest level
  named, and each stand-in after it.
- **LAN play asked for by name** (a game on their own home network): the build may make the small
  server it needs, and the line says `Online: LAN on your own network, as you asked: not checked
  (the self-check plays one browser)`.
- **The stand-in never proves the online game.** A bot is not a person and a slider is not real
  lag: the stand-in shows whether the idea is fun, not whether it holds up over a network.

## VR

`Platform: VR: Claude Code Game Studios has no route for VR: no checks and no proof that a VR
game works. Build it makes a flat-screen game for the browser instead.`

It is built flat only on Build it with this line on the card; Change something asks what they
would like instead. With nobody there, it is built flat and the line is *provisional*.

## A phone

`Platform: phone: it runs in a phone's browser, with no touch kit yet: touch controls are built
from your words, and no check plays them.`

A tap or a swipe they name is built; the self-check still plays with keys.

## A console

`Platform: console: a console port goes through a licensed partner or a porting house (the
console makers' kits are private, so Claude Code Game Studios cannot build or check one); this
web game is where it starts.`

Their goal stays as they said it; the web game is built as for any other.

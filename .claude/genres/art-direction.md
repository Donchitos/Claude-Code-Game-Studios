# Art direction: one written for each game

The card reads this file once per game; the builder never loads it. There is no menu of looks:
you write a short art direction for this game alone, from the person's words, their pictures and
what the genre's best current games look like, never a style picked off a list.

## The rules

- **Their words and pictures win.** A look they name is built as named, in its own words on the
  brief's Look line: "2.5D" is a 3D world under a fixed tilted camera, or 2D layers with depth and
  parallax, never a flat top-down view or a free 3D camera; "flat vector" is flat vector;
  "like Manor Lords" takes that game's camera, proportions, palette mood and light (grounded,
  earthy, soft daylight, small figures in a large world), never its art itself. A look that
  needs art code draws badly (painted, hand-drawn, realistic, hi-bit pixel) is still built
  toward the direction, never swapped for another look.
- **No default look.** Never the flat cream background with bare shapes: nobody asks for it.
  Nothing said about the look is not a licence for the plainest one: pick a direction
  from the genre's list below, near the hook and the person's words, and mark it *guessed*.
  Two games are never given the same direction by habit: choose for this game.
- **Never an emoji or a font symbol as art**, on a figure, a tile, an icon or the HUD. Draw it.
  Only when the person asks for emoji by name.
- **Every enemy kind and every unit has its own silhouette**, readable at play size: a different
  outline, not one shape recoloured or resized. The player reads apart from all of them.
- **Not a copy.** A named game guides the direction; its characters, logos and exact art stay its
  owner's (an Avoid line on the brief).

## The form

One line each, in the brief's Look and references:

- **Mood:** the pitch in a few words, and the feeling the screen gives.
- **Camera and scale:** the view, the native resolution and its scale rule (whole-number for pixel).
- **Shapes:** the shape language of the player, the hazards and the world (round and soft, sharp
  and angular, blocky).
- **Values:** the player and the hazards brightest or darkest, the background held in a narrower range.
- **Palette:** a few colours with roles (player, threat, ground, accent) and a mood, with the
  light and shadow hue shift (shadows cooler, lights warmer, or the person's own).
- **Detail:** where detail goes (faces, edges, the hook) and where it is kept out (behind play).
- **Rules:** one pixel density, one outline rule, one shading model.
- **Signature:** one detail no other game here has: a lamp glow, a paper edge, a brush texture.

## Failures to avoid

Smoothing left on for pixel art · a camera that moves in sub-pixels · two pixel sizes on one
screen · outlines run over noisy meshes · a preset palette used uncurated, with no roles.

# Chipset: cozy-cleanup

**Camera:** `static`, one room or object that fits the window; `first` with the tool in hand, a
game like PowerWash Simulator; `orbit` around one object.
**Research:** needs primary research: our research has one cleaning-simulator row, drawn from
PowerWash Simulator and Unpacking. It has no entry for choosing among tools, such as a nozzle or a
sponge for each kind of dirt.

## Card defaults

- **clear-finish**: A bar fills as the mess goes, and each object or area is ticked off when it is
  done. Why: the job can be finished, and the player sees how far there is to go. Break it when:
  free decorating with no right answer, a room arranged as you like.
- **forgiving-finish**: When an object is nearly clean the last specks clear themselves, and a hint
  shows where a missed spot is. Why: finishing never becomes a hunt for an invisible speck. Break it
  when: the last speck is the challenge, a game about precision.
- **no-pressure**: No timer and no move that costs: anything placed can be picked up again, and
  nothing breaks. Why: the player settles into the task. Break it when: a timed cleaning rush, a
  race to tidy before the guests arrive.

## Feel

- **every-stroke-shows**: Each stroke of the tool clears the mess under it at once, with a sound
  that changes as the surface comes clean. Why: each action visibly reduces the mess, the heart of
  the genre. Break it when: tidying is about where things go, not scrubbing.
- **snap-into-place**: An item dragged near its spot slides into it with a soft click, and things
  sit neatly on shelves. Why: placing feels tidy without fiddly aim. Break it when: a little mess is
  the charm, a cozy clutter arranged by hand.
- **done-chime**: Finishing an object flashes it, rings a small chime and ticks it off, and the
  whole job ends on a bigger one. Why: each small finish is a reward on the way to the big one.
  Break it when: one quiet reveal at the end is the point.
- **before-after**: The finished space is shown before and after, or as a short replay of the
  clean-up. Why: the player sees how much they changed. Break it when: the mess keeps coming back,
  an endless tidy.

## Feel variants

- **scrub**: Scrub and spray: one tool, one big dirty thing, a lot of surface to clear.
- **unpack**: Unpack and sort: items from boxes to where they belong, a quiet puzzle of a life.
- **arrange**: Arrange: small scenes to set just right, each with a neat answer to find.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The job:** wash a dirty thing clean (default) · unpack and sort a room · set small arrangements
  just right · decorate a room however you like.
- **Stakes:** none, no timer and nothing lost (default) · a star rating for a quick or tidy finish ·
  paid jobs that buy better tools.
- **What the player sees:** one room or object, `static` (default) · first person with the
  tool, `first` · around one object, `orbit`.
- **Feeling:** the player feels order from chaos by watching each stroke clear the mess, because
  every action visibly reduces disorder and the finish is forgiving.

## Later, never in a one-shot

- A life story told by what is in the boxes → grow it
- Many rooms or jobs in a row → grow it
- Better tools bought with job pay → change it after play
- Furniture from a shop to decorate with → add it as a feature
- Cleaning together with a friend on one screen → change it after play
- Dirt that comes back over time → change it after play

## In a mashup

Cleanup leads the mess, the tools and the finish. A farming or life sim leads the day, and tidying
is one chore in it. A detective game leads the clues, and cleaning turns them up. Horror leads the
dread when the mess is a crime scene. Management leads the money when the jobs are paid.

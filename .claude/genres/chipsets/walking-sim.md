# Chipset: walking-sim

There is no tested way to build large authored and branching stories yet: a one-shot is one small place
(a house, a lookout, a shore) and one story found in it.

**Camera:** `first`; `orbit` behind a figure you can see, `fixed` for a set angle that follows.
**Research:** needs primary research: our research has about four walking-sim entries, on reveals,
travel and small discoveries that chain into a mystery; the other lines lean on environmental
storytelling, level design and sound. It has no entry for notes, diaries or recorded voices, or for
the speed of the walk itself.

## Card defaults

- **story-trail**: The story is found in order: something wrong is in view from the start, a trail
  of traces leads on, and the last piece ends the walk on an end screen. Why: the player pieces it
  together, and the ending lands because they found their way to it. Break it when: the walk is pure
  mood with no story, a calm stroll.
- **lines-while-walking**: Narration and voices appear as a line of text at the bottom while you
  keep walking, never in a box that stops you. Why: the story never stops the walk. Break it when:
  the story is all in objects and notes, with no voice at all.

## Feel

- **gentle-walk**: Walking is slow and smooth, holding a key goes a little faster, and a gentle sway
  of the view can be turned off in the settings. Why: the place is seen, not rushed, and no one
  feels sick. Break it when: the walk is a chase, a thriller where speed matters.
- **lead-the-way**: Light, paths and one landmark seen from the start pull the walk on, and the next
  room shows from this one. Why: the player never feels lost and never needs an arrow. Break it
  when: getting lost is the point, a maze or a dream.
- **pick-up-and-turn**: Things worth a look glow faintly when you look at them; picking one up
  brings it close to turn over, with its own sound. Why: the story is in the objects, and holding
  one makes it real. Break it when: nothing is touched, a walk told only by voice.
- **reveal-the-space**: A narrow way in opens onto a wide view, and the music swells at the opening.
  Why: the reveal is the walk's reward, felt before it is understood. Break it when: the whole walk
  is one small room.
- **footsteps-and-air**: Footsteps sound different on wood, grass and stone, and each room has its
  own quiet background sound. Why: the place feels solid and lived in with nothing on screen. Break
  it when: the look is abstract and silent on purpose.

## Feel variants

- **wistful**: Quiet and wistful: a slow walk through a lived-in home, small objects, soft music.
- **uneasy**: Uneasy: rooms change a little when you turn back, sounds come from places you left,
  and nothing ever attacks.
- **awe**: Wide and awed: a long walk across open land toward a huge landmark, wind and distance.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **Where:** one house, room by room (default) · open land walked toward a landmark · a place that
  loops and changes each time round · the sea floor, open above and below.
- **How the story comes:** objects and notes you find (default) · lines that play as you reach
  places · a voice on a radio you answer, for which there is no tested way to build yet.
- **What the player sees:** your own eyes, `first` (default) · a figure you follow from behind,
  `orbit`.
- **Feeling:** the player feels quiet discovery by finding the last piece of a story in a place,
  because each object they held added a piece.

## Later, never in a one-shot

- Talking back to a voice, with choices → add it as a feature
- Many rooms and a long story → grow it
- A place that changes for what you have already seen → add it as a feature
- A list of found things to collect → change it after play
- Sound muffled through walls and doors → change it after play
- Input from the body, such as blinking → not tested yet: ask for it after play and the AI will try it untested
- A final action that sums up the story → change it after play
- A threat that hunts you through the place → add it as a feature

## In a mashup

The walking sim leads the pace, the place and the order the story is found in. A detective half
leads the evidence and what the player must work out. A puzzle half leads its rules, and the story
leads what solving them means. Horror leads the threat and the dread. A cozy or tidying half leads
gentle handling, and the story leads what the objects mean.

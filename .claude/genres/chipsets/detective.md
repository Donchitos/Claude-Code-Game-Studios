# Chipset: detective

A one-shot is one case: one scene or a few rooms, a handful of clues and one deduction. The
case is kept fair and solvable; there is no tested way to build many cases or suspects who talk back yet.

**Camera:** `static`, one drawn scene; `first` for a room you walk through in 3D.

## Card defaults

- **notebook**: Every clue you find goes into a notebook you can open at any time with the Notes
  button, in the words you will need for the answer. Why: nothing found is lost, and clues are
  compared side by side. Break it when: the whole case fits on one screen, every clue in view at
  once.
- **fill-the-blanks**: The answer is built, not picked from a list: fill blanks such as who did
  what, to whom and how, from names and words you found. Why: a right answer proves the player
  worked it out. Break it when: the person wants an accuse-the-suspect finale, one pick from a
  line-up.
- **hints-on-ask**: A Hint button points to where to look, never to what it means. Why: a stuck
  player gets moving and the deduction stays theirs. Break it when: the person wants it hard, with
  no help.

## Feel

- **look-closely**: Any spot in the scene can be looked at closer; the view eases in, and a real
  clue chimes and flies into the notebook. Why: the player is the one who spots it, and the find is
  felt at once. Break it when: the clues are all in testimony, an interrogation with nothing to look
  at.
- **confirm-in-groups**: The game says correct only when a few linked answers are right together,
  and that moment plays a clear sting. Why: guessing one at a time does not work, so a confirmation
  is a real eureka. Break it when: the case is one question with one answer.
- **case-retold**: Solving the case ends on a results screen that retells what really happened, step
  by step, and ends on who did it. Why: the player sees their reasoning come true, a high to end on.
  Break it when: the mystery is meant to stay open, a story that never explains.

## Feel variants

- **cozy**: Cozy and puzzling: no clock, no penalty, a tidy case at your own pace.
- **tense**: Tense, a thriller: a clock runs, a suspect may get away, and the sound tightens as time
  runs out.
- **loop**: Time loop: the same few minutes repeat, and only what you learned carries into the next
  loop.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The case:** one scene frozen at the moment it happened, read for who did what (default) · a time
  loop where knowledge opens the way · recorded interviews searched by word.
- **How it is solved:** fill in the blanks from found words (default) · accuse one suspect at the
  end · catch a lie by showing the clue that breaks it.
- **Stakes:** no penalty, try as often as you like, answers confirm in groups (default) · a few
  wrong accusations, then the case goes cold · a clock.
- **What the player sees:** one drawn scene, `static` (default) · walk the room in your own
  eyes, `first`.
- **Feeling:** the player feels independent deduction by naming who did it from clues no one
  explained, because the game confirms only when the reasoning is right.

## Later, never in a one-shot

- A case that turns midway into a different problem → add it as a feature
- Several cases and a story across them → grow it
- Suspects who answer questions, and lie → add it as a feature
- A board of leads that links places and clues → add it as a feature
- Revisiting a place that has changed → add it as a feature
- A city of suspects who live their own lives → not tested yet: ask for it after play and the AI will try it untested
- Endings that depend on when you face the culprit → add it as a feature

## In a mashup

The detective leads the evidence, the notebook and what counts as solved. A metroidvania or an
action adventure leads movement and the map, and knowledge can open a door the way a tool does. A
visual novel leads the talks with suspects. A cozy half leads gentle handling, and hints never give
the answer away. A walking sim leads the place and the order it is explored in.

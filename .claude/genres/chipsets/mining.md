# Chipset: mining

**Camera:** `follow2d`, a side view that follows the digger down; `topdown` for digging from
above.
**Research:** needs primary research: our research has one mining entry, tool tiers that gate
materials. The fuel, the bag and the trip back up to sell rest on survival and push-your-luck
entries from other genres, and no entry comes from a game about digging down and selling the haul.

## Card defaults

- **dig-sell-upgrade**: Dig down, fill the bag, ride back up to sell, then buy a better drill, a
  bigger bag or a bigger tank; the shop shows what each upgrade does, and each costs more than the
  last. Why: the whole loop is clear from the first trip. Break it when: there is no way back up,
  one long dive to the bottom.
- **fuel-runs-down**: Fuel runs down as you dig and shows on screen beside how full the bag is;
  running dry hauls you to the surface with part of the haul lost. Why: each trip is a choice of how
  deep to go before turning back. Break it when: a calm dig with no limit.
- **deeper-richer**: Each layer down looks different and holds better ore and harder rock, and a
  rock the drill cannot break yet says what it needs. Why: the locked rock gives the next upgrade a
  purpose. Break it when: a short arcade dig with one kind of rock, where the route is the game.

## Feel

- **dig-crunch**: Each block cracks in steps under the drill, with its own sound and a spray of
  flakes: dirt goes fast, stone takes a moment, and each drill upgrade cuts visibly faster. Why: the
  hundredth block still feels good to break. Break it when: digging is one click per block, a puzzle
  of where to dig.
- **ore-glints**: Ore glints in the rock a little before you reach it, and picked-up ore flies into
  the bag with its own chime. Why: the player steers toward the next find, and every find is a small
  win. Break it when: finding ore is the mystery, and a scanner upgrade shows it.
- **sell-rush**: Back at the surface, the bag empties one ore at a time with the money counting up.
  Why: the trip's payoff lands in one rush. Break it when: ore sells the moment it is dug, with no
  trip back.

## Feel variants

- **steady**: Calm and steady: plenty of fuel, dig and sell at your own pace, every tunnel stays as
  you left it.
- **greedy**: Push your luck: fuel goes fast, the best ore lies deep, and every trip is a gamble on
  how far to go.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The trip:** dig down and ride back up to sell (default) · dig between waves, then race up to
  defend the base on the surface · one long dive to the bottom, no way back.
- **Stakes:** running dry costs part of the haul (default) · falls and lava hurt the hull, and a
  wreck ends the run · no limit, dig at your own pace.
- **The ground:** generated from a seed each game (default) · one
  hand-made mine with secrets.
- **What the player sees:** a side view, `follow2d` (default) · from above, `topdown`.
- **Feeling:** the player feels greedy tension by digging one block deeper for a rare ore with the
  tank nearly dry, because the way up is long and the haul is safe only once it is sold.

## Later, never in a one-shot

- Monster waves to defend between digs → add it as a feature
- New tools that open new materials and regions → grow it
- Explosives, cave-ins and falling rock → change it after play
- Drills that mine on their own while you dig → add it as a feature
- Starting over deeper with a bonus, a prestige reset → add it as a feature
- Hidden rooms, relics and a story underground → grow it

## In a mashup

Mining leads the dig, the trip and the haul, and the economy prices the upgrades. A tower-defense
half leads the waves, and mining pays for the defences. A survival half leads needs and the base. A
platformer half leads movement and jumps in the tunnels. A roguelite half leads the run and what
carries over after a wreck.
